# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Retail and first-time investors in Nepal who want to trade on NEPSE, open DEMAT/broker accounts, apply for IPOs, and manage their portfolio. Secondary audiences include existing clients looking up forms, branch locations, market data, and support. Users are often on mobile, may have limited financial literacy, and need clear reassurance that the broker is SEBON-licensed and trustworthy before sharing personal documents or opening an account.

## Product Purpose

Imperial Securities' public website exists to build trust, explain services, and move qualified visitors toward account opening (online KYC or branch visit) and ongoing client self-service (downloads, FAQs, market tools, external portal links). Success means a visitor understands who Imperial is, feels confident in its regulation and track record, and can complete their next step without confusion — whether that is opening an account, finding a form, or reaching support.

## Brand Personality

**Trustworthy · Established · Clear**

Voice is professional but approachable — a regulated broker, not a startup or crypto platform. Copy should be direct and factual (license numbers, branch count, settlement terms) rather than hype-driven. Visual design should feel institutional and calm: confidence through clarity, not flash. The site represents a company operating since the late 1990s with 200,000+ clients; it should read as durable and competent, not experimental.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI template**: Inter/system fonts, purple-to-blue gradients, identical card grids, tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows on every section, numbered section markers used as decoration, gradient text, decorative glassmorphism, hero-metric blocks (big number + small label + stats row).
- **Startup SaaS**: Bounce/elastic easing, excessive motion, pill badges everywhere, nested cards, gray muted body text on tinted backgrounds that fails contrast, "delight" micro-interactions that distract from regulated financial content.
- **Flashy fintech / crypto**: Neon accents, terminal-dark aesthetic, gamified UI, aggressive gradients, novelty typography — inappropriate for a SEBON-licensed broker serving everyday Nepali investors.
- **Dated legacy broker**: Cluttered tables, tiny type, no mobile polish, PDF-first workflows with no digital path, walls of undifferentiated links.

## Design Principles

1. **Regulation is a feature** — SEBON license, broker number, NEPSE/CDSC membership, and compliance paths (grievance, disclosures) should be easy to find, not buried in footnotes.
2. **Clarity over decoration** — Every section earns its place. Prefer editorial layout and strong hierarchy over card grids and icon tiles when explaining services or company story.
3. **One clear next step** — Each page should answer "what do I do now?" (open account, download form, call branch, read FAQ) without competing CTAs.
4. **Digital-first, branch-backed** — Online KYC and tools are primary; physical branches and phone support are visible fallbacks, not the default path.
5. **Identity preservation** — Extend the existing blue/green OKLCH brand system and Syne + DM Sans pairing; do not introduce off-palette accent colors (purple, indigo, cyan) for one-off sections.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** for all public pages. Body text must meet ≥4.5:1 contrast; large headings ≥3:1. All form fields require programmatic label association. Interactive targets ≥44×44px on touch devices. Support **`prefers-reduced-motion`**: provide instant or crossfade alternatives for Framer Motion and CSS animations. Do not rely on color alone for market direction or status. Nepali/English bilingual needs may grow; keep copy plain and avoid idioms that do not translate.
